## The problem

Automating short-form video is easy to do badly. The obvious version scrapes popular videos, cuts them up and re-uploads them at volume. That is copyright infringement and the fastest way to get a channel struck under YouTube's reused-content and spam policies. I wanted to see what an automated pipeline looks like when the guardrails are the design, not an afterthought: it should only touch content it has rights to, refuse near-duplicates, disclose synthetic media, put a human in front of AI-generated scripts, and default to low volume.

## How it works

`shorts-engine run` executes one day of the pipeline; `daemon` repeats it until the campaign ends.

1. **Trends.** `trends.py` finds the most-viewed recent uploads for niche keywords through the YouTube Data API, drops off-topic hits, and scores keywords by view velocity, with extra weight from the channel's own top performers.
2. **Sources.** `sources.py` only uses what the rights registry in `config.yaml` lists: own videos dropped in `data/inbox/`, or licensed URLs downloaded with yt-dlp that carry a recorded `permission_ref`.
3. **Highlights.** faster-whisper produces a transcript with word timings. `highlights.py` cuts 20 to 58-second windows and scores them for hook, pace and trend match. An optional LLM pass re-ranks them and writes the title, hook, commentary and tags.
4. **AI originals.** With an LLM configured, `original.py` scripts a short on a trending topic, narrates it with TTS and lays captions over owned b-roll.
5. **Guardrails.** `guardrails.py` checks rights and, with the default thresholds, rejects clips that overlap an earlier clip from the same source by more than 50%, rejects transcripts above 0.6 Jaccard similarity on 3-word shingles, and rejects duplicate normalised titles.
6. **Render.** ffmpeg produces 1080×1920 output with a blurred fill background, word-by-word highlighted ASS captions, hook text, a credit line, a spoken commentary intro and audio normalised to −14 LUFS.
7. **Review and upload.** AI originals wait until `shorts-engine review --approve <id>`. Uploads are resumable, always `private` with a `publishAt` time spread across the publish window, and stop cleanly when the API quota is hit.
8. **Stats.** Views, likes and comments for the last 14 days of uploads feed back into trend scoring.

## Engineering decisions

**Rights as data, enforced in code.** The registry is configuration and the check is code. A licensed source with no permission reference is refused rather than warned about, because a warning in a daemon log is not a control.

**Cheap, explainable duplicate detection.** Shingle Jaccard and interval overlap are simple and fast, and when they reject a clip I can say exactly why. Embeddings would catch more paraphrase, but a guardrail that cannot explain itself is hard to tune.

**Volume is the last lever, not the first.** The ramp grows linearly from 1 to 3 uploads a day over 14 days, and `plan` prints the day-by-day quota before anything runs. Each source yields at most a configured number of clips per day and earlier clips are never reused. The README says it bluntly: more sources raise volume, turning off guardrails does not.

**Identity checks on every batch.** `auth` refuses to log in as any channel other than the configured `channel_id`, and every upload batch re-checks it, so a wrong browser session cannot publish to the wrong channel.

**Degrade without an LLM.** Without one, clips still work with heuristic scoring and the first sentence as the title. AI originals and spoken commentary are skipped rather than faked.

**Operational honesty about Google.** The README documents the two restrictions that will bite: uploads from unaudited API projects stay private, and refresh tokens for "Testing" OAuth apps expire after seven days.

## Results & evidence

- **18 offline pytest tests** (16 functions, one parametrised over three degenerate ramp shapes). They cover the duplicate guardrail, the rights registry, the ramp and quota maths, publish-slot spacing, config validation, captions, highlight scoring and trend aggregation. Heavy optional dependencies are skipped with `importorskip`, and render tests skip without a libass-enabled ffmpeg.
- CI runs the suite on Python 3.10, 3.11 and 3.12.
- `doctor` checks every setup step, and `run --dry-run` renders and shows the schedule without uploading.

I report no channel performance numbers; the project is about the pipeline and its controls.

## Limitations & next steps

- **Early-stage.** It works end-to-end for a single owner and channel, but interfaces and config keys may change. The README says so in a badge at the top.
- **No pipeline integration tests yet.** The tests cover the logic units; the orchestration in `pipeline.py` is not tested against mocked YouTube and LLM clients.
- **Guardrails do not guarantee policy compliance.** They reduce risk; the operator still has to read and follow YouTube's policies.
- **Clips skip human review** by default; only AI originals are held.
- **Next:** integration tests, licence metadata with expiry and scope, a local review UI, optional review for clips, a policy lint for titles and descriptions, and a container image with ffmpeg and libass.
